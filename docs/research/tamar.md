## 1. Assessment Types: Formal vs Informal

### 1.1 Definitions (p.6, §1.3.1)

- "There are two authorized Types of Assessment Methods, described as Formal and Informal." (§1.3.1.1)
- **Formal:** "A Formal Assessment has a sufficient degree of rigor and corroboration which can result in a formal maturity rating against the model." (§1.3.1.1.1)
- **Informal:** "An Informal assessment does not require any corroboration of sources and will not result in a formal maturity rating against the model." (§1.3.1.1.2)
- Choice of type: "dependent upon their requirements … Assessments can be utilized for subtly different purposes throughout an organization's long-term strategy." (§1.3.1.2)
  - Guidance: "Assessment methods may be used for internal process improvement; to establish the organization's maturity level; and for the purpose of selecting suppliers of particular services (e.g. by stating that any supplier dealt with must be certified as a TMMi Level 3 organization)."
- Hierarchy: "methods which conform to the Formal Assessment criteria will also fulfill the requirements for Informal Assessments." (§1.3.2.1)
- "All Assessment methods submitted for accreditation should fall into one of these two Types." (p.7, §2.1.1.2)

Glossary (p.22):
- **formal assessment** — "the more rigorous of the two Types of Assessment, leading to a fully verifiable benchmark rating. Each process component must be corroborated by interview and artifact reviews, with the option to collect additional evidence."
- **informal assessment** — "the less rigorous of the two Types of Assessment. Each process component is supported by one source only, which may not validated" [sic].
- **assessment type** — "Level of assessment rigor; either Formal or Informal".

### 1.2 Criteria tables (p.7, §2.1.1.3)

**Table 1 — Formal Assessment Criteria**

| Team Leader | Team Size | Evidence Collected | Maturity Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accredited Lead Assessor | At least 2 | "Staff interviews and documents required. Other types of corroborative evidence can also be used – e.g. questionnaires, customer surveys, etcetera (see section 2.2.3.7 for Organizational Scope requirements)" | "Verifiable benchmark rating of the organization (as defined in the scope of the Assessment) against TMMi is produced, with areas of strength and weakness identified in fine detail, and full gap analysis presented" |

**Table 2 — Informal Assessment Criteria**

| Team Leader | Team Size | Evidence Collected | Maturity Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experienced Assessor | At least 1 | "One type of non-corroborative evidence – e.g. staff interviews, documents, questionnaires, customer surveys, etcetera – is required" | "No rating against TMMi is produced. Utilized purely for 'quick check' Assessments to gain a rough understanding of an organizational area's maturity level – thus useful for internal Assessments" |

"Note: For both Types of Assessment, the 'Assessment Team Size' includes the Assessment Team Leader." (p.7)

### 1.3 Common to both types (p.8, §2.2.2)

- "The higher levels within the TMMi reference model [TMMi] contain the lower levels. Therefore, for example, to obtain a Level 3 achievement, all Level 2 practices and goals must also be fulfilled." (§2.2.2.2)

### 1.4 Formal Assessments in detail (p.8–9, §2.2.3)

- Leader: "Formal Assessments must be led by an accredited Lead Assessor. Lead Assessor accreditation can only be achieved through the TMMi Foundation." (§2.2.3.1)
- Team: "must consist of a Lead Assessor … and at least one other accredited Assessor." (§2.2.3.2)
- "Additional Assessment Team Members other than the Lead Assessor and first Assessment Team Member must be experienced as Assessors, but not necessarily accredited (but may be unaccredited trainee Assessors)." (§2.2.3.3)
- "Formal Assessments require a higher level of proof and corroboration for the achievement of Practices and Goals, Process Areas, and Maturity Levels within the TMMi than Informal Assessments do." (§2.2.3.4)
- "A wider variety of data from multiple sources must be gathered during a Formal Assessment." (§2.2.3.5)
  - "It is mandatory to collect data from staff interviews conducted by the Assessment Team and corroborate the information gathered in the interviews by gathering documents and other written materials." (§2.2.3.5.1)
  - "Other types of evidence (for example, Customer Surveys) may be gathered – which should corroborate each of the other data sources." (§2.2.3.5.2)
- "The data must be drawn from different, representative roles within different parts of the organization, in order to examine whether practices are institutionalized." (§2.2.3.6)
- Data coverage mechanism required (§2.2.3.7) — see Section 3.3 below.
- Re-certification / moving up a level (§2.2.3.7.4): the Lead Assessor "shall ensure that all requirements for the original assessment scope are still being satisfied."
  - Guidance: a certificate "is valid for a maximum 3 years. At the end of this period, the certificate lapses or the organization will need to be re-assessed … and the certificate may be extended for a further 3 years." When assessing at a higher level, "the assessment for any higher TMMi maturity level needs to take the previously assessed process areas into account." "It is the responsibility of the Lead Assessor to demonstrate the implementation of previously assessed process areas continues to be satisfactorily implemented…"
- Results: "The results for a Formal Assessment will show strengths and weaknesses of an organization against TMMi in depth, presenting a full gap analysis." (§2.2.3.8)
  - Guidance: "The assessment method procedures and outputs shall enable the Assessment Sponsor to identify the individual organizational strengths and weakness at practice level. The assessment method procedures may additionally enable identification of strengths and weaknesses at sub-practice level."
- The method "shall require the Assessment team to notify the TMMi Foundation in advance of the commencement of a Formal Assessment by informing the Accreditation Chair." (§2.2.3.9)
- The Lead Assessor must "provide full information (to be included in the Assessment DSR) to justify the scope for the assessment as being representative of the organizational entity under assessment." (§2.2.3.10, p.8–9)

### 1.5 Informal Assessments in detail (p.9, §2.2.4)

- "Informal Assessments may be performed with less rigor than is required by for [sic] a Formal assessment." (§2.2.4.1)
- "designed as an initial indicative view and 'quick check' to see the current snapshot state of an organizational area's processes, rather than as a full and formalized evaluation." (§2.2.4.2)
- "Informal Assessments should be led by an experienced Assessor. However, the Assessor does not need to be formally accredited to perform the role." (§2.2.4.3)
- "The Assessment Team for an Informal Assessment can consist of a single person. This corresponds with the aim of Informal Assessments being quick, low-impact evaluations." (§2.2.4.4)
- "allows an organization to form an indicative view on whether or not their current practices and procedures comply with the TMMi." (§2.2.4.5)
- "The only requirement for an Informal Assessment is for just one type of evidence to be supplied by the area being examined." (§2.2.4.6)
  - Guidance: "There is a lower need to validate and verify evidence received, as that would not satisfy the main objective of this level of Assessment, which is of speed and low business impact, rather than high levels of rigor."
- "for this level of Assessment, any evidence should be accepted as it stands, and no attempt at corroboration will be made." (§2.2.4.7)

### 1.6 Can an informal assessment give a maturity level rating? — **No.**

- "will not result in a formal maturity rating against the model" (p.6, §1.3.1.1.2)
- "No rating against TMMi is produced." (p.7, Table 2)
- "Process ratings apply to Formal Assessments and process findings (which are not rated) apply to Informal Assessments." (p.12, §3.2.8.2 guidance)
- "An Informal Assessment cannot give any formal rating from the Assessment (as defined by the Scope at the start of the Assessment) but should provide findings highlighting process strengths and weaknesses." (p.16, §3.5.2.7)
- The compliance table (p.18–19) marks all rating clauses §3.4.1.1, §3.4.1.3, §3.4.1.4, §3.4.2.1–§3.4.2.7, §3.4.3.1–§3.4.3.5 as **Formal: Yes / Informal: No**; §3.4.1.2 ("TMMi defines guidance as to goal achievement") is Yes/Yes.

## 2. Assessment Process and Roles

**[Note]** TAMAR does not define a numbered phase model. Its requirement structure is: Planning (§3.2) → Data Management (collection + validation, §3.3) → Process Component Rating (§3.4) → Reporting & Outputs (§3.5). The case study (Annex 4) adds a pre-report findings presentation to participants. TAMAR maps to ISO/IEC 33002 (§3.1.1, Chapter 5).

### 2.1 General method requirements (p.10, §3.1)

- Methods "should comply with current published version of TAMAR … which interprets the international standard ISO/IEC 33002 … all TMMi Assessment Methods which comply with the requirements detailed within TAMAR will conform to ISO/IEC 33003 as interpreted." (§3.1.1.1)
- "All Assessments must be conducted in accordance with a documented method." (§3.1.2.1). Guidance: processes and procedures documented and "accompanied by templates".
- Terminology must be used consistently (§3.1.3.1). Guidance: "the terms … Not Achieved, Partially Achieved, Largely Achieved and Fully Achieved shall not be modified in the Assessment Method materials, because changes to these definitions could cause inconsistencies in assessment results."

### 2.2 Planning (p.10–12, §3.2)

- "A plan for the Assessment shall be formulated prior to its initiation." (§3.2.1.1); "formally documented" (§3.2.1.2); "distributed to, and agreed by, all key stakeholders" (§3.2.1.3) — at minimum "the Assessment Sponsor and the Assessment Team" (§3.2.1.3.1).
- "The Assessment Sponsor must appoint a User-side Assessment Organizer, to manage the Assessment in accordance with the documented Assessment Plan." (§3.2.1.3.2). Guidance: "The Assessment Sponsor and the Assessment Organizer can be the same person, if required."
- "Key stakeholders and others will be fully briefed as appropriate at commencement of the Assessment." (§3.2.1.3.3)
- Plan contents: "activities, resources, schedules, responsibilities, success criteria, and a description of the intended Assessment outputs." (§3.2.1.4). Formulated by the Assessment Team Leader (§3.2.1.5).

**Five inputs to the Assessment Plan (§3.2.2, p.10–11):**
1. **Purpose** — "the first and primary input … why the Assessment is being carried out."
2. **Scope** — "how wide-ranging the Assessment is, which organizational elements have been included to ensure representative coverage, and what Type of Assessment is being performed." (see Annex 1)
3. **Constraints** — "availability of key resources; the maximum duration of the Assessment; specific organizational areas to be excluded from the Assessment; and any confidentiality agreements in place that may control the release of information."
4. **Method** — "the identity of the method that will be utilized."
5. **Assessor Competence Criteria** — "the levels of competence required by the assessment team and verifies that the assessment team meets the minimum competency requirements. This is guided by the Type of Assessment being conducted."

- Activities (§3.2.3): depend on the method; must enable assessing against TMMi and producing the required outputs.
- Resources (§3.2.4): people (team members, sponsor, organizer, interviewees) and items "such as room bookings".
- Schedule (§3.2.5): "finalized and agreed before the initiation"; states "the date, time, location, and attendee list for all planning meetings, interviews, data-gathering sessions, feedback sessions, and results presentations."
- Success criteria (p.12, §3.2.7.1): "relate to the satisfactory fulfillment of the Assessment Plan, and the Assessment complying with this document to the required level".
- Intended outputs (p.12, §3.2.8.2): "the Assessment date; the Assessment inputs; identification of all objective evidence gathered throughout the Assessment; identification of the documented Assessment process followed; and the set of process ratings or findings resulting from the Assessment."

### 2.3 Roles (p.11–12, §3.2.6) — "There are three main roles"

**Assessment Sponsor (§3.2.6.2)**
- "a person who is internal to the organization, which is being assessed, and who is providing resources and impetus for the Assessment to take place." (§3.2.6.2.1)
- "responsible for verifying that the Assessment Team Leader is competent to perform an assessment which conforms to the TMMi reference model [TMMi] and the documented assessment method." (§3.2.6.2.2)
- May delegate part of the role, e.g. to the Assessment Organizer (§3.2.6.2.3).
- (Or Organizer) arranges schedule / coordinates internal resources, e.g. interviews (§3.2.6.2.4); is "the primary point of contact for the Assessment Team" (§3.2.6.2.5); receives the results on behalf of the organization (§3.2.6.2.6).
- "The Assessment Method shall recognize the Assessment Sponsor as the owner of the assessment data." (§3.2.6.2.7). Guidance: the Sponsor "must be made aware of and grant permission for: data to be collected; data to be maintained in secure storage; and data to be submitted confidentially to the TMMi Foundation. Note: This does not imply that the Assessment Sponsor may request the source of the individual data items and thereby circumvent the assessment confidentiality procedures as described in section 3.3.1."

**Assessment Team Leader (§3.2.6.3, p.12)** — for Formal = accredited Lead Assessor
- "will manage all aspects of the Assessment" (§3.2.6.3.1); present throughout all planning meetings (§3.2.6.3.2); "participates in all Process Area rating reviews" (§3.2.6.3.3); has input into all feedback sessions and results presentations (§3.2.6.3.4); "presents the final results to the Assessment Sponsor (or nominated Assessment Organizer)" (§3.2.6.3.5).
- Provides evaluation feedback on participating Assessors and trainees (§3.2.6.3.6). Guidance: "not required to participate in all interviews, but should at least attend a sufficient number of interviews in order to assess the performance of the participating Assessors."
- "shall submit a detailed report on the results or findings of the assessment" (§3.2.6.3.7). Guidance: content "shall comply with the requirements as defined in the TMMi Data Submission Requirements."
- Reports on participating Assessors to the TMMi Foundation for accreditation evidence (§3.2.6.3.8).
- "shall ensure that secure, confidential storage is provided for the archival of the assessment data and evidence for a minimum of three years." (§3.2.6.3.9)

**Assessment Team Member (§3.2.6.4, p.12)**
- Present at planning meetings as appropriate; attends interviews "performing a role of either an interviewer, or as a scribe, or both" (§3.2.6.4.2).
- "Individual interview ratings for Practices need to be decided by consensus of at least two Assessment Team Members (for Formal Assessments)." (§3.2.6.4.3)
- Participates in all PA rating reviews (§3.2.6.4.4); input into feedback sessions and results presentations (§3.2.6.4.5).

**Glossary role definitions (p.22–23)**
- assessment organizer — "either filled or delegated by the Assessment Sponsor … to organize the Assessment on the user-side".
- assessment team leader — "For a Formal Assessment, this must be an accredited and trained Lead Assessor".
- assessment team member(s) — "The Assessment Team Leader is also an Assessment Team Member. People who are Assessment Team Members are also known as Assessors".
- assessor(s) — "any member of the Assessment Team, whether they are a Lead Assessor or an Assessment Team Member."
- experienced assessor — "Assessor with demonstrable working experience of undertaking formal evaluations against models (may be unaccredited Trainee Assessor)."
- lead assessor — "accredited by the TMMi Foundation as competent to lead and participate in any Type of Assessment".
- trainee assessor — "may be inexperienced in Assessments, and thus is not yet accredited as a full Assessor".
- Re-certification — "Assessment being conducted to certify an organizational unit after an initial assessment has been successfully conducted at the same or a lower Maturity Level."

## 3. Evidence Rules

### 3.1 Types of evidence / instruments (p.7, §2.1.2)

"there are several types of instruments (evidence) … this is not inclusive; other instruments may be identified." (§2.1.2.1)
- **Staff interviews** — "a mandatory part of Formal Assessments, are conducted by the Assessment Team, which makes a formal record of the information gathered from such sessions." (§2.1.2.1.1)
- **Documents** — "supplied to the Assessment Team in support of Practices. The Team will look at the structure, content and implementation/usage of the documents." (§2.1.2.1.2)
- **Evaluation Questionnaires** — "structured tools to gather evidence about Practices from individuals within the organization being assessed." (§2.1.2.1.3)
- **Customer surveys** — "may also be used as a form of corroborative evidence, although in this case the evidence will come from outside of the organization being assessed." (§2.1.2.1.4)

Data collection methods (p.13, §3.3.1.2): "confidential submission of documents"; "confidentially interviewing people who define, utilize or are otherwise involved with a particular process"; "distribution and confidential collection of evaluation questionnaires"; "confidentially surveying the customers of a particular organization."

**[Note]** TAMAR does **not** use the terms "direct vs indirect" artifacts or "affirmations" (those are SCAMPI/CMMI terms). Its distinction is interviews vs documents/other written materials vs questionnaires vs customer surveys, and corroborative vs non-corroborative evidence.

### 3.2 Corroboration requirements

- **Formal:** interviews are mandatory and must be corroborated by documents: "It is mandatory to collect data from staff interviews conducted by the Assessment Team and corroborate the information gathered in the interviews by gathering documents and other written materials." (p.8, §2.2.3.5.1). Other evidence "should corroborate each of the other data sources" (§2.2.3.5.2). Glossary: "Each process component must be corroborated by interview and artifact reviews, with the option to collect additional evidence." (p.22)
- §3.3.2.2 guidance (p.13): "For Formal Assessments: Staff interviews and documents are required. Other types of corroborative evidence may also be used in support of evidence gathered from staff interviews and documents. For Informal Assessments: Any single type of evidence, e.g. staff interviews, documents, questionnaires, customer surveys etcetera".
- **Informal:** "no attempt at corroboration will be made" (p.9, §2.2.4.7); "one source only, which may not validated" (p.22).
- **[Note]** TAMAR does not specify a numeric count of independent sources (e.g. "two sources"); the rule is the pairing *interview + documents/written materials* for Formal, plus data drawn from "different, representative roles within different parts of the organization" (§2.2.3.6). The only "at least two" numbers are: team size ≥2 (Formal), practice interview-rating consensus by ≥2 team members (§3.2.6.4.3), and ≥2 projects per organizational unit in the last 12 months (Annex 1 rule 6).

### 3.3 Data coverage (Formal) (p.8, §2.2.3.7)

The method "shall require a defined mechanism for determining that sufficient data has been collected to cover the scope of the Formal assessment" per Annex 1:
- §2.2.3.7.1: "A practice has sufficient data coverage if corroborated objective evidence exists for the practice and is:
  - Adequate to understand the extent of implementation of the practice
  - Representative of the organizational unit
  - Representative of the life-cycle phases in use within the organizational unit as defined in Annex 1".
- §2.2.3.7.2: "A process area has sufficient data coverage if all of its practices have sufficient data coverage."
- §2.2.3.7.3: "Practices have sufficient data coverage if there is sufficient evidence to validate the consistency of the data for each of the practices."

### 3.4 Data validation (p.13, §3.3.2)

- "All data collected must be able to be verified as objective evidence." (§3.3.2.1) [Formal only]
- "All data collected must be sufficient and representative for the purposes of the scope of the Assessment and shall meet the minimum requirements defined in Annex 1" (§3.3.2.2) [both]. Guidance: "The assessment method shall define a process for selecting sufficient samples of data to ensure that the data set is representative for the organization unit."
- "All data collected must be examined to check that it is consistent: With each of the other sources of data; As a whole as evidence of the implementation for each practice." (§3.3.2.3) [Formal only]
- Origin of evidence recorded "to enable later verification of the Assessment results" (§3.3.1.3) [Formal only].

### 3.5 Confidentiality and non-attribution (p.12–13, §3.3.1)

- Data "must be collected in a manner, such that: The sources of the all [sic] data are confidential; The data is non-attributable to any individual" (§3.3.1.1.1–2) — **required for both types**.
- Guidance (§3.3.1.3): "The assessment method shall define a process (and a template) to ensure that the data used to determine each assessment result must be traceable. This process shall ensure that the source of the data is confidential and non-attributable to any individual."
- Sponsor owns the data but may not request individual sources (p.11, §3.2.6.2.7 note).
- Constraints input includes "any confidentiality agreements in place that may control the release of information" (§3.2.2.4).
- Secure confidential storage for ≥3 years (§3.2.6.3.9).
- Case study (p.35): Lead Assessor reasserts "that the source of the data supporting the findings shall remain confidential."

### 3.6 Organizational scope & sampling (Annex 1, p.25–27)

Main factors (§A1.3, p.25): "Number of delivery models implemented"; "Number of and different size of projects delivered in representative period before the assessment"; "Number and size of teams".

Guidelines the Assessment Lead "must" consider (p.25–26):
1. "A minimum of 1 project for each delivery model must be included in the scope of the assessment"
2. Consider number of geographical locations; projects from sufficient locations so all teams follow equivalent processes. "Organizational units that are specific to customers in a particular geographical region should be assessed in separate formal assessments. Multi-site project teams delivering work to the same customer may be treated as one project team."
3. "A minimum of 1 project for each domain must be included" (domain = group within the unit, e.g. billing, eCommerce; or customer-facing group, e.g. Property & Casualty, Life).
4. "A minimum of 1 project for each delivery model of each size must be included in scope".
5. Number of projects delivered during the preceding 12 months taken into account.
6. "A minimum of 2 projects delivered within the last 12 months for each organizational unit in the assessment scope must be in scope of a formal assessment." Notes: number "statistically significant and representative"; "Each major release of the same application should be considered as a separate project for sampling decisions."
7. "It is sufficient to select 1 project delivered within the last 12 months for each development model and organizational unit."
8. "Supporting artefacts must be collected from all projects identified as in scope of the assessment"

"Note: The eventual scope of the assessment will involve consideration of all of the above as they are inter-related and should not be applied in isolation."

**Sample Size Formula (p.26):** "Minimum Number of projects to be selected for each organizational unit in the scope of the assessment: The greater of (2 or [Sum of (Domains, Development Methods & Project Sizes) + Log10(Number of projects)])". Normal rounding ("5.49 shall become 5; 5.5 shall become 6"). The Lead Assessor may define additional sampling factors (documented in the Plan) "so long as the overall number of projects selected is not reduced." The team "must document the analysis and justification for the scope". "The Sponsor may choose to reduce the scope of the assessment by reducing the number of organisation units in scope".

Examples (p.26):

| Domains | Project Sizes | Development Methods | # Projects | Sample Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Medium | Traditional, Agile | 31 | 7 |
| 1 | Small | Agile | 120 | 5 |
| 1 | Large, Medium | Traditional | 20 | 5 |
| 2 | Large, Medium, Small | Traditional, Agile | 75 | 9 |

**[Note]** check: row 1 = 3+1+2 + log10(31)=1.49 → 7.49 → 7; row 4 = 2+3+2 + 1.88 → 8.88 → 9.

**Exclusion from scope / NA rules (§A1.4, p.26–27)** — "primarily applicable to formal assessments"; "a rating of 'NA' should therefore only be used sparingly if the relevant Process Area is genuinely not applicable":
1. "'Not Applicable' shall not be used to rate either individual Practices or Goals provided that the alternate practices satisfy the requirements and the intent of the Goal."
2. "A Process Area may not be rated as 'Not Applicable' if the Sponsor and/or Lead Assessor exclude the Process Area from the scope of the assessment, Process Areas deemed to be out of the scope of the assessment shall be rated 'NR'."
3. If justifiable "and provided that no more than a single Process Area within each Maturity Level is rated 'Not Applicable', the Assessment Result shall then state the Maturity Level was attained with Exceptions to make this exception transparent. If more than one (1) Process Area within each Maturity Level is rated 'Not Applicable', then the Maturity Level is rated as 'N', i.e. 'Not Achieved'"
4. "Overall, no more than two (2) Process Areas may be rated as 'Not Applicable' within the scope of the assessment (except where the scope of the assessment is restricted to Maturity Level 2 where only one (1) Process Area may be rated as 'Not Applicable')."

## 4. Rating Rules (§3.4 + Annex 2)

### 4.1 Rating scale (p.13–14, §3.4.1, §3.4.2.1)

"The level to which an organization achieves a particular process goal shall be measured using a scale which consists of the following levels: N (Not Achieved), P (Partially Achieved), L (Largely Achieved), and F (Fully Achieved)." (§3.4.1.1)

| Ordinal | Meaning | Practice achievement |
|---|---|---|
| **N** | Not Achieved | **0% to ≤15%** |
| **P** | Partially Achieved | **>15% to ≤50%** |
| **L** | Largely Achieved | **>50% to ≤85%** |
| **F** | Fully Achieved | **>85% to 100%** |

Nominal ratings: **NA** — Not Applicable; **NR** — Not Rated.

- "The ratings N, P, L and F are applicable to the four levels at which ratings can be applied, namely: Practices, Goals, Process Areas, and Maturity Levels). **The associated percentage achievement ratings are applicable to Practices only.**" (p.14)
- "The ratings NA and NR are applicable in accordance with the rules below."
- Rationale for rating decisions "shall be recorded" (§3.4.1.3); "All process component ratings must be traceable to the particular objective evidence that was used to score that component rating." (§3.4.1.4). Guidance: templates to ensure "traceability between the evidence and the process area, goal and practice."

### 4.2 Definitions per level (p.14–15, §3.4.2.2–§3.4.2.5)

**N — Not Achieved (§3.4.2.2):** "there should be little or no evidence found of compliance with the TMMi."
- Practices: "exhibit substantial weaknesses in implementation and/or institutionalization and no adequate alternative exists. The practice achievement is in the range of 0% to ≤15%."
- Goals: "supporting practices which exhibit substantial weaknesses … and no adequate alternative practices exist. Typically the vast majority of the supporting practices are rated either 'N' (Not Achieved) or 'P' (Partially Achieved)."
- Process Areas: "exhibit at least one Goal rated N."
- Maturity Levels: "exhibit at least one Process Area rated N."

**P — Partially Achieved (§3.4.2.3):** "there should be some evidence found of compliance with the TMMi. The practices and processes are incomplete, not widespread, or inconsistent in application or results."
- Practices: "significant weaknesses in implementation and/or institutionalization or a limited alternative practice exists. The practice achievement is in the range of >15% to ≤50%."
- Goals: "significant weaknesses … and limited alternative practices exist. Typically the vast majority of the supporting practices are rated either 'P' (Partially Achieved) or 'L' (Largely Achieved) or the supporting practice ratings vary widely including 'F' (Fully Achieved) and 'N' (Not Achieved). In case the rating of the goal is higher than the lowest rating of the supporting practices, an explicit rationale shall be provided for the rating of the goal. The rationale shall be documented in the assessment report."
- Process Areas: "at least one goal rated P and all other Goals rated F or L."
- Maturity Levels: "at least one Process Area rated P and all other Process Areas rated F or L."

**L — Largely Achieved (§3.4.2.4):** "there should be significant evidence found of compliance with the TMMi. The practices and processes are both systematic and widespread. However, there may still be some minor weaknesses in the distribution, application, or results of the practices and/or processes."
- Practices: "only minor weaknesses in implementation and/or institutionalization with no significant negative impact on the intent of the practice or an appropriate alternative practice exists, which is exhibits [sic] only minor weaknesses with no significant negative impact on the intent of the practice. The practice achievement is in the range of >50% to ≤85%."
- Goals: "only minor weaknesses … with no significant negative impact on the intent of the goal or appropriate alternative practice(s) exist … Typically the vast majority of the supporting practices are rated either 'L' (Large Achieved) or 'F' (Fully Achieved) and no supporting practices are rated 'N' (Not Achieved)." + same explicit-rationale rule if goal rated higher than the lowest supporting practice.
- Process Areas: "at least one goal rated L and all other Goals rated F."
- Maturity Levels: "at least one Process Area rated L and all other Process Areas rated F."

**F — Fully Achieved (§3.4.2.5, p.14–15):** "there should be consistent convincing evidence found of compliance with the TMMi. The practices and processes are both systematic and widespread and there are no obvious weaknesses in the distribution, application, or results of the practices and processes."
- Practices: "only trivial weaknesses (if any) in implementation and institutionalization or a wholly appropriate alternative practice exists. The practice achievement is in the range of >85% to 100%."
- Goals: "no weaknesses in implementation and institutionalization or a wholly appropriate alternative practice(s) exist with no negative impact on the intent of the goal. Typically the vast majority of the supporting practices are rated 'F' (Fully Achieved) and no supporting practices are be [sic] rated 'P' (Partially Achieved) or 'N' (Not Achieved)." + same explicit-rationale rule.
- Process Areas: "exhibit all Goals rated F."
- Maturity Levels: "exhibit all Process Areas rated F."

### 4.3 NA and NR (p.15, §3.4.2.6–§3.4.2.7)

- **NA:** "to be used if a Process Area is not applicable in the context of the Organization and Organization Unit being assessed as defined according to the rules in Annex 1 … and is therefore excluded from the results." Guidance: "A Process Area is not applicable if there is no reasonable requirement for the practices comprising the process area to exist in the context of the organizational unit. A Process Area is not rated NA because a key stakeholder has chosen to exclude it from the scope of the assessment."
- **NR:** "to be used if the process component cannot be rated due to insufficient or inconsistent evidence or falls outside the scope of the assessment." Guidance: "A Process Area which the sponsor has chosen or has agreed with the Lead Assessor for some reason to exclude from the scope of the assessment should be rated NR."
  - Practices and Goals "must be rated NR if: the Process Area falls outside the scope of the assessment; the assessors cannot reach consensus; or insufficient data has been gathered to enable the practice or goal to be rated." (§3.4.2.7.1)
  - Process Areas "must be rated NR where a Process Area is deemed out of the scope of the assessment or where any supporting goal is rated NR" (§3.4.2.7.2)
  - Maturity Levels "must be rated NR where any supporting Process Area is rated NR." (§3.4.2.7.3)
  - Guidance: "The assessment method shall define a process for resolving issues where practices and goals are not rated to ensure that process area and maturity level ratings can be determined."

### 4.4 Determining ratings — aggregation (p.15, §3.4.3)

- **Practice:** "guided by the TMMi reference model [TMMi] and the procedures defined in the assessment method such that the assessment team achieves consensus on the Practice's rating." (§3.4.3.2)
- **Goal:** "guided by the ratings for all of the supporting practices … such that the assessment team achieves consensus on the Goal's rating." (§3.4.3.3). Guidance: "If a TMMi Goal (G) is supported by 3 Practices (P) then the rating for the G shall be dependent on the ratings for the 3 Ps in accordance with the procedures defined in the assessment method." → **Goal rating is a consensus judgement, not strictly the minimum** (see §3.4.2 "typically the vast majority…", explicit rationale required if goal > lowest practice; R1.2 revision note p.4).
- **Process Area:** "The rating for each Process Area shall be equivalent to the lowest rating of the Goals that support the Process Area." (§3.4.3.4)
- **Maturity Level:** "The rating for each Maturity Level shall be equivalent to the lowest rating of the Process Areas that support the Maturity Level. A Maturity Level may be rated as achieved by the organization if all Process Areas in scope have been rated as either Largely Achieved or Fully Achieved. Furthermore higher maturity level cannot be achieved without lower levels also being achieved." (§3.4.3.5)
  - Guidance: "The ratings for Process Area (PA) and Maturity Level are automatically determined by the ratings for each of the supporting Goals, such that the lowest rating for the Goals determines the ratings for the PAs and the Maturity Level."

### 4.5 Annex 2 — Rating Process Areas and Maturity Levels (p.28–30), in full

"This annex elaborates on how measurements are applied for process areas and maturity levels … using examples".

#### 2.1 Rating Process Areas (p.28)

"Process Area ratings are equivalent to the lowest rating of the supporting goals, as defined in section 3.4.3 … Note: The process area rating is 'only as strong as the weakest link', i.e. the process area will be rated at the lowest rating of the supporting goals."

For the process area to be:
- **Fully Achieved** — "The goals associated with the process area are all rated as Fully Achieved"
- **Largely Achieved** — "The goals associated with the process areas are all either Fully or Largely Achieved, and at least one goal is only Largely Achieved"
- **Partially Achieved** — "The goals associated with the process area are all either Fully, Largely or Partially Achieved, and at least one goal is only Partially Achieved"
- **Not Achieved** — "The goals associated with the process area are all either Fully, Largely, Partially or Not Achieved and at least one goal is Not Achieved."
- **Not Rated** — "Any supporting goal is Not Rated or the Process Area has been excluded from the scope of the assessment."
- **Not Applicable** — "The process area is considered not applicable to the organizational unit by the Lead Assessor, i.e. there is no reason for this process area to be implemented or institutionalized. See Annex 1 … for rules regarding the usage of this rating."

Guidance: "If a process area is in scope for an assessment, then all goals and practices should be in scope; otherwise the expected practices for the process area are likely to be only partially implemented."

| Example | Goal ratings | PA rating | Explanation (verbatim gist) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1.1 Ex. 1 | G1 L, G2 L, G3 F, G4 F, G5 F | **L** | "The lowest rating of the goals is 'Largely Achieved' (for G1 and G2); therefore the overall rating for the process area is 'Largely Achieved'." |
| 2.1.2 Ex. 2 | G1 L, G2 L, G3 F, G4 P, G5 L | **P** | lowest is P (G4) → PA "Partially Achieved". |
| 2.1.3 Ex. 3 | G1 L, G2 F, G3 F, G4 P, G5 NR | **NR** | "The lowest rating of the goals is 'Partially Achieved' (for G4). However, note that G5 is 'Not Rated', therefore the process area cannot be rated and the overall status of the process area is 'Not Rated'. The assessment team would be required to reach agreement on the rating for G5 and may possibly need to gather more data to reach consensus on the rating." (p.28–29) |

#### 2.2 Rating Maturity Levels (p.29)

"Maturity Level ratings are equivalent to the lowest rating of the supporting process areas … Note: The maturity level rating is 'only as strong as the weakest link', i.e. the maturity level will be rated at the lowest rating of the process areas."

For the maturity level to be:
- **Fully Achieved** — "The process areas associated with the maturity level are all rated as Fully Achieved"
- **Largely Achieved** — "… all either Fully or Largely Achieved, and at least one process area is only Largely Achieved"
- **Partially Achieved** — "… all either Fully, Largely or Partially Achieved, and at least one process area is only Partially Achieved"
- **Not Achieved** — "… all either Fully, Largely, Partially or Not Achieved, and at least one process area is Not Achieved"
- **Not Rated** — "Any supporting process area is Not Rated"

| Example | PA ratings | ML rating | Outcome stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.2.1 Ex. 1 (p.29) | ML2: PA1 F, PA2 L, PA3 L, PA4 F, PA5 F, PA6 L | ML2 = **L** | "the organization is operating at TMMi Maturity Level 2." |
| 2.2.2 Ex. 2 (p.29) | ML2: PA1 F, PA2 L, PA3 L, PA4 P, PA5 F, PA6 F | ML2 = **P** | "the organization is operating at TMMi Maturity Level 1." |
| 2.2.3 Ex. 3 (p.30) | ML2: PA1 F, PA2 F, PA3 L, PA4 F, PA5 **NA**, PA6 F | ML2 = **L** | "PA5 is 'Not Applicable' in the organization's context and is therefore does not influence the result of the assessment … operating at TMMi Maturity Level 2. An explanatory note stating why PA5 is deemed 'Not Applicable' must be provided." |
| 2.2.4 Ex. 4 (p.30) | ML2: PA1 F, PA2 L, PA3 L, PA4–6 F; ML3: PA1–PA5 all F | ML2 = L; ML3 = **L** ("see note below") | "While the lowest rating of the process areas at level 3 is 'Fully Achieved', the overall rating for maturity level 3 is 'Largely Achieved', since the rating at maturity level 2 was 'Largely Achieved'. The organization unit has achieved maturity level 3 overall." |
| 2.2.5 Ex. 5 (p.30) | ML2: PA1 F, PA2 L, PA3 L, PA4 P, PA5 F, PA6 F; ML3: PA1 F, PA2 L, PA3–5 F | ML2 = **P**; ML3 = **P** | "While the lowest rating of the process areas at level 3 is 'Largely Achieved' (for PA2), the overall rating for maturity level 3 is 'Partially Achieved', since the rating at maturity level 2 was 'Partially Achieved'. The organization has achieved maturity level 1 overall." |

### 4.6 Derived rules (summary)

- **"Satisfied" PA:** TAMAR does not use the word "satisfied". The operative rule: a Maturity Level "may be rated as achieved … if all Process Areas in scope have been rated as either Largely Achieved or Fully Achieved" (§3.4.3.5). So a PA effectively counts toward a maturity level when rated **L or F**; PA rating = lowest goal rating.
- **Maturity level (staged):** ML rating = lowest PA rating at that level **and** is capped by the rating of lower levels (Annex 2 Ex. 4–5); "higher maturity level cannot be achieved without lower levels also being achieved" (§3.4.3.5); "to obtain a Level 3 achievement, all Level 2 practices and goals must also be fulfilled" (§2.2.2.2). ML achieved iff all in-scope PAs (at that and all lower levels) are L or F.
- **NA:** PA excluded from the result (does not influence the ML rating), with explanatory note; ≤1 NA PA per ML (else ML = N); ≤2 NA overall (≤1 if scope limited to ML2); ML "attained with Exceptions" stated; NA never used for Practices/Goals or for sponsor-chosen exclusions.
- **NR:** propagates upward — any NR goal ⇒ PA NR; any NR PA ⇒ ML NR. Out-of-scope (sponsor exclusion) PA ⇒ NR.
- **Lowest-rating rule:** applies mechanically to PA (from goals) and ML (from PAs). **Not** mechanically to goals (from practices): the goal is a consensus rating; if higher than the lowest supporting practice, an explicit rationale must be documented in the assessment report.

## 5. Annex 4 — Assessment Case Study (p.33–35)

### 5.1 Purpose (p.33, §A4.1)
"to illustrate aspects of the application of the TAMAR rules as part of the assessment method." It "purposely does not cover all aspects", covering "planning the scope of a (formal) assessment, selecting the sample of projects to be included in the assessment scope, evidence review to determine assessment ratings and the reporting of the findings."

### 5.2 Background (p.33, §A4.2)
- **You-Buy-IT** is launching an own-brand software range; marketing wants "a price promise backed by high quality products". A new software division was created 18 months ago. The IT Director wants a review of IT practices "to assess capabilities of the new division and to establish a baseline for future improvements"; positive results → marketing wants "independent certification of the IT processes in their marketing campaigns".
- **ABC Testing Services**, an accredited TMMi Service Provider, is engaged by **Mr James Smith** (senior manager reporting to the IT Director, responsible for internal development and testing) "to formally assess the Own Brand Software Delivery Division's test processes." ABC had previously done "periodic informal assessments" and continuous improvement consultancy for You-Buy-IT.

### 5.3 Planning steps (p.33, §A4.3)
1. Mr Smith = **Assessment Sponsor**; after agreeing objectives and considering feedback from the continuous improvement programme, agrees scope = **TMMi maturity levels 2 and 3**. He selects an accredited Assessment Provider, which nominates a **Lead Assessor**.
2. "The Lead Assessor informs the TMMi Foundation Accreditation chair of the planned formal assessment." (cf. §2.2.3.9)
3. **NA decision for PA 2.5 Test Environment:** environments are cloud-based, provisioned by a single supplier under a master contract with SLAs (provision within 4 hours, penalties for breaches). Lead Assessor and Sponsor "debate this and finally agree" PA 2.5 is "not applicable": "the environment requirements are standardized, allowing automated builds to create a new environments on demand. Furthermore, all the division's software operates without the need to integrate with other systems, therefore test data management is not deemed to be a significant issue to manage. Test environment incidents are managed by the supplier in accordance with the contracted SLAs." Justification noted in the Assessment Plan.
4. **Scope/sampling:** division treated as a single operational domain; uses Agile for small projects and RUP for medium and large; 20 projects completed in last 12 months + 5 at advanced testing (due within 8 weeks) = 25. Team types/sizes correlate with project types/sizes, so not an additional sampling factor (noted in Plan).
5. **Sample size:** "(1 domain + 2 development methods + 3 project sizes) + Log10(25 projects) = 6 + 1.39 which is rounded to 7." Lead Assessor agrees with Sponsor "that a minimum of 7 of the 25 projects should be included in scope".

### 5.4 Evidence review & rating derivation (p.34–35, §A4.4)
- Interviews done and documents/artifacts gathered "for each of the 9 process areas for each of the 7 projects in scope". [Note: 10 PAs at ML2+3 minus PA2.5 NA = 9.]
- Lead Assessor: "this is not a mathematical exercise where scores can be summed to calculate an average." Team must reach consensus; review supporting practices for each goal "and determine whether the intent of the goal has been satisfied in the context of the Own Brand Software Delivery Division operations and the objectives for the assessment"; may consider "which practices are relatively more important".
- Worked example: **PA 2.3 Test Monitoring and Control, practices 3.1, 3.2, 3.3** (supporting goal G3).

Initial indicative ratings per project:

| | P1 Med RUP | P2 Large RUP | P3 Large RUP | P4 Med RUP | P5 Small Agile | P6 Small Agile | P7 Large RUP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P3.1 | L | F | F | L | L | NR | F |
| P3.2 | F | F | F | F | L | P | F |
| P3.3 | L | F | L | L | L | NR | L |

- Consistency/corroboration check: Agile projects 5 and 6 had limited documentary evidence, preventing consensus for project 6. "If this is not resolved the assessment team would be unable to rate P3.1 and P3.3 and also G3." After debate with the Lead Assessor, the team agree "the use of scrums, the output of sprint and project retrospectives, and the associated action plans are sufficient for the purposes of reaching consensus." Project 6 re-reviewed → L for all three practices.

Revised ratings: identical except P6 = L, L, L.

- Overall practice ratings (consensus, "not a mathematical exercise"):
  - **P3.1 = F** — projects 1, 4, 5, 6 show minor institutionalization weaknesses of a common issue-analysis procedure; RUP vs Agile procedures differ, but team agrees the approach "is wholly appropriate with respect to the intent of the practice".
  - **P3.2 = F** — minor weaknesses in projects 5 and 6 do "not impact on the organization unit's overall capability".
  - **P3.3 = L** — all projects except project 2 fail "to consistently determine the effectiveness of the corrective actions".
  - Each decision documented with "the rationale for their decision, the practice strengths, the minor weaknesses and the cross references to the supporting data".
- **Goal G3 = L** "documenting the rationale … the goals strengths, the minor weaknesses and the cross references to the supporting interview and artifact data". Lead Assessor notes this "will not prevent the Sponsor from achieving the objective achieving public certification" provided no other significant weaknesses.
- PA 2.3 goals: G1 F, G2 F, G3 L, G4 F, G5 F → "the Process Area rating is effectively equivalent to the lowest rating of all the goals, therefore the rating for PA2.3 is 'L'". "if the issues with the ratings for project 6 we [sic] left unresolved G3 would be rated 'NR' and PA2.3 would be rated 'NR'."

### 5.5 Reporting (p.35, §A4.5)
1. **Findings presentation to participants (before final report):** Lead Assessor presents findings to the division members who participated, "reasserting that the source of the data supporting the findings shall remain confidential." Participants told "what key strengths and weaknesses were identified for each goal for each process area" with an opportunity to feed back comments and queries. "Crucially the assessment team do not share the assessment ratings, but simply confirm that their findings are seen to be fair by the participants attending the presentation."
2. Team finalizes the written report once confident findings "fairly reflect the projects' practices".
3. Final ratings:

| PA | 2.1 | 2.2 | 2.3 | 2.4 | 2.5 | 3.1 | 3.2 | 3.3 | 3.4 | 3.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rating | F | F | F | L | NA | F | F | F | F | F |

   "Maturity Level 2 is rated 'L' … and Maturity Level 3 is rated 'L' … due to the minor weakness at Level 2, even although all the Process Areas at Level 3 were rated 'F'."
   **[Note]** the table shows PA2.3 = F and PA2.4 = L, whereas §A4.4 derived PA2.3 = L — an internal inconsistency in the PDF; the ML outcome (L) is the same either way.
4. Report content: "findings highlighting the key strengths and the key weaknesses with respect to the requirements of the TMMi and assessment report includes recommendations and proposals on options available to the Sponsor to address the process weaknesses." Justification for PA2.5 "NA" "clearly documented in the report and the assessment data submission to the TMMi Foundation."
5. Final check that method requirements are met and the TMMi Foundation data submission is "complete and correct".
6. Report submitted to the Sponsor, "congratulating him on achieving Maturity Level 3 and reminds him that the results must still be accepted by the TMMi Foundation before a certificate is issued and the results are made public on the TMMi Foundation's website."

## 6. Reporting Findings, Strengths/Weaknesses, Recommendations

- "All Assessment results shall be formally documented." (p.16, §3.5.1.1) [Formal only]; "made available to the Assessment Sponsor (or nominated Assessment Organizer)." (§3.5.1.2) [both]
- **Output Report must contain (p.16, §3.5.2):**
  1. "the date of the Assessment" (§3.5.2.2)
  2. "a list of the Assessment inputs gathered during the Planning stage … This list shall include justification of the scope selected by the Assessment Lead in the Assessment Plan." (§3.5.2.3)
  3. "a list of the objective evidence gathered during the Data Collection stage" (§3.5.2.4) [Formal only]
  4. "details of the Assessment method used" (§3.5.2.5)
  5. "for Formal Assessments, an overall rating from the Assessment (as defined by the Scope at the start of the Assessment)" (§3.5.2.6)
  - Informal: no formal rating, "but should provide findings highlighting process strengths and weaknesses." (§3.5.2.7)
- Formal results show "strengths and weaknesses … in depth, presenting a full gap analysis" (§2.2.3.8); strengths/weaknesses identifiable "at practice level", optionally "sub-practice level" (guidance, p.8). Table 1: "areas of strength and weakness identified in fine detail, and full gap analysis presented".
- Team Leader "presents the final results to the Assessment Sponsor" (§3.2.6.3.5); submits a detailed report per the DSR (§3.2.6.3.7); team members/leader have input into "all feedback sessions and results presentations" (§3.2.6.3.4, §3.2.6.4.5); schedule must include "feedback sessions, and results presentations" (§3.2.5.2).
- Goal-rating rationale: when a goal is rated higher than its lowest supporting practice, "an explicit rationale shall be provided … documented in the assessment report" (§3.4.2.3.2, §3.4.2.4.2, §3.4.2.5.2).
- NA: explanatory note required (Annex 2 Ex. 3); ML "attained with Exceptions" must be stated (Annex 1 rule 3).
- Recommendations: TAMAR's normative text does not mandate recommendations; the case study shows the report "includes recommendations and proposals on options available to the Sponsor to address the process weaknesses" (p.35).
- Certification: results must be accepted by the TMMi Foundation before a certificate is issued and published (p.35); certificate valid max 3 years (p.8).
